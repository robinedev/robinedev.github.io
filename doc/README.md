# How to work with me

This page is the shortcut, so you don't have to work me out by trial
and error. It covers how work reaches me, how it gets planned, how
you'll hear about it while it's happening, and where I'll probably
need a nudge.

If you only read one line, make it this one: nothing gets built before
you've seen it written down.

## What happens when you send a request

You'll get to see what I understood before I go off and build it.

That's usually a quick sketch or a short write up sent back your way,
so there's a chance to catch anything I've picked up wrong, and those
ten minutes have saved me weeks of rework over the years.

Send it over however's easiest for you, whether that's a paragraph, a
quick call, or a rough visual mockup. Diagrams are what really stick
in my head, so if you've already got one I'd love to see it, and if
you haven't, I'm more than happy to draw one up and walk through it
with you.

For anything with real consequences I like to write it up as an RFC,
so the reasoning is out in the open and everyone's got room to weigh
in properly. The
[HashiCorp template](https://www.hashicorp.com/en/how-hashicorp-works/articles/rfc-template)
is my usual starting point, and I'm just as happy to use whatever your
team already runs on.

## When to catch me

Mornings are for building and afternoons are for people, so if you
want heads-down work out of me it's happening before lunch, and if
you'd rather think out loud together then grab an afternoon.

Honestly, the people half is the part I look forward to! Bouncing an
idea around, hearing how your team actually works day to day, finding
out what's been quietly driving everyone up the wall: that's the fun
of this job, not the tax on it.

## What gets looked at first on a new team

I start with a gap analysis across configuration and endpoints,
because it's worth knowing what's genuinely out there rather than what
everyone assumes is out there. After that I'm really chasing two
questions:

- **What did nobody write down?** There's usually one person quietly
  carrying it around in their head, and that's a risk to them far more
  than it is to anyone else.
- **What happens fifty times a month?** If it's repetitive it should
  be automated, and I'd love to take it off your hands.

All of it goes somewhere visible, whether that's Jira or whatever you
already use, so anyone waiting on a thing can go and look rather than
having to chase me for an update.

## Where this goes wrong

Two honest ones, and they've got the same fix.

**I run too many threads at once.** When several projects are live,
the quiet one is always the one that slips. If yours has gone quiet,
please nudge me, because it's never a lack of care, it's a lack of a
reminder.

**I go quiet when I'm heads-down.** When I'm deep in a build it's far
too easy to forget that silence reads as "nothing's happening" to
everyone waiting on me. Ask me for an update whenever you want one,
and I mean that, you'll never be bothering me.

## How to tell me something is wrong

Plainly, early, and one to one, because that's the version I can
actually do something about.

If it's something bigger, send it over in writing first so I've had a
chance to sit with it properly, and then let's talk it through.
Nothing needs softening on the way in either. Saying it straight is
the kind thing to do.

## What you get at the end

**It still works once I've moved on.** It's either automated or
documented, and ideally it's both.

**One owner, not five.** Endpoints, identity, access reviews, and all
the evidence an audit asks for. You won't need to project manage me.

**Bad news early**, including the times when the mistake is mine. The
config was wrong, not the person who ran it.

## Reaching me

| channel | good for | you can expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ticket | anything with a deliverable | same working day |
| Chat | quick unblocks, "is this normal?" | a few hours |
| Email | external, formal, anything with a paper trail | a day or two |
| Meeting | decisions, conflict, onboarding, demos | happy to, with an agenda and a written recap after |

And if your thing has to wait its turn, you'll hear that from me
rather than hearing nothing at all.

## The short version

**Fills the tank:** a messy problem with room to redesign it, a
whiteboard with someone else standing at it, learning how another team
works, and the pure joy of finally deleting a process nobody needed!

**Empties it:** meetings with no agenda, and decisions that nobody
owns.

And if something's outside my lane, you'll hear that from me too,
along with the name of someone better placed to help.
