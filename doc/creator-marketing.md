# Creator and community marketing, for dev tools

If you're reading this, I sent you the link. It isn't in the nav anywhere, because I'd rather hand it to people than leave it sitting on my front page.

Short version: I'm just starting out freelance in SaaS content, and I haven't taken a creator deal from first DM to final cut yet. I'd rather say that now than have it come up later. Everything below is work I actually did.

I do startup IT the rest of the week, which turns out to be the useful part. I'm writing for people who do my job. I've also been the admin on the paying account for tools like these, so I've seen the part of a product that a demo doesn't show you.

### What I write for Fleet

Fleet is open source device management, and I contribute there. Most of what I write is industry news, which goes like this: a CVE lands, every vendor publishes roughly the same post, and the interesting part is finding the angle that gets someone who has already read ten of them to read the eleventh.

Three I'd point at: [the one about a single asterisk leaving Alpine Linux open for ten years](https://fleetdm.com/industry-news/sshhole-openssh-alpine-password-bypass), which went out the same week BleepingComputer and CSO Online covered it. [The one arguing your Windows patch report can't see Microsoft Paint](https://fleetdm.com/industry-news/microsoft-paint-cve-2026-83844-default-apps), which Dark Reading and Cybernews picked up. And [a quieter one on Firefox Enterprise](https://fleetdm.com/industry-news/firefox-enterprise-what-it-means-for-it) for admins deciding whether to manage a second browser.

I've written the last three monthly newsletters too, [September](https://fleetdm.com/newsletters/newsletter-2026-09), [October](https://fleetdm.com/newsletters/newsletter-2026-10), and [November](https://fleetdm.com/newsletters/newsletter-2026-11). If you'd rather see everything than my highlights, [the full list of what I've contributed](https://github.com/fleetdm/fleet/pulls?q=is%3Apr+author%3Arobinedev) is public.

### The one I'm happiest with

Fleet's launch posts are usually release notes and a link. I pitched one as a two panel comic instead: a swan in a suit, being asked what exactly got upgraded.

[It got](https://lnkd.in/p/eQed9RYR) 13 reactions, 2 comments, and a repost, which is fine but not the point. The part I care about is that Fleet's CEO showed up in the comments to add detail rather than just liking it. That's the bar I use now. If the person who built the thing wants to add to the joke, the joke was carrying something true.

There's also [an ad I wrote and art directed](https://www.linkedin.com/ad-library/detail/1572308543), a rabbit in a field of daisies holding up some genuinely technical claims. 1,000 to 5,000 impressions, no boost behind it. Soft visual, real promise underneath. That trade is the whole job.

### Where I actually am

I put Fleet's newsletter straight into the MacAdmins Slack, a few thousand IT admins who would scroll past a company account without noticing it. I'm on Reddit most days as well, more lurker than poster, which is deliberate. I'd like to know what a room responds to before I say anything in it.

### What I'd like to do next

I have a handful of ideas I haven't shown anyone yet. Some are tutorials, some are short form, and at least one isn't a video at all. A few lean on how people actually watch and play now, Discord native drops and speedrun style challenges, rather than a trailer cut down into shorts.

Give me 15 minutes and I'll walk you through five of them, shaped around what you're building rather than recycled from somewhere else. If none of them land, you keep them.
