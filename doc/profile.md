# About me

I'm an IT engineer in Columbus, Ohio. I live where device management
meets identity, and I like the unglamorous half of the job: the part
where a new hire opens a laptop that already knows who they are, and
nobody had to touch it first.

## How I got here

I started where a lot of us start, on a help desk with a queue and a
stack of laptops. I got annoyed enough at repeating myself that I
learned to script my way out. That turned into fleet management, which
turned into identity, which turned into the security and compliance
work that pays the bills now.

The thread running through all of it: take the thing a human does
fifty times a month, and make it happen once, correctly, on its own.

## What I actually do

- **Endpoints.** Zero touch enrollment, configuration as code, and
  patch and compliance baselines that report on themselves.
- **Identity.** Joiner, mover, and leaver automation, SSO
  integrations, and access that's scoped narrowly and expires without
  a reminder.
- **Security operations.** Hardening, evidence collection, and turning
  an audit into a report instead of a fire drill.

## Some things I've shipped

At TravelPerk I led IT for North America. When we acquired AmTrav, I
took it from signed deal to 300+ devices live in Intune and Jamf on
day one, and led the IT half of our SOC 2 Type 1 certification.

At HashiCorp I ran client platform engineering across 2,000+ devices
and 200+ software assets, with automated integrations doing the asset
and compliance tracking that people used to chase by hand.

Now I'm head of IT at FuturHealth, building the whole function for a
growing remote team. I also contribute to Fleet, and got certified at
the MacAdmins conference this year.

## Outside the terminal

**Pickleball.** The sport that looks gentle until someone sets up at
the kitchen line and you realise you've been drawn into a chess match
at close range. I play more than I probably should.

**Foraging.** I like knowing what's edible in a patch of woods I walk
past every week. It's the same instinct as the day job, honestly:
learn the system well enough to read it at a glance, and be careful
about the bits that will hurt you if you guess.

**Gardening.** Slower feedback loops than anything at work, which is
the point. Nothing in a garden can be fixed by restarting it.

**Trading card games.** Deckbuilding is just systems design with
better art. I will happily talk about a format I haven't played in
years.

And tea. Strong, usually over-steeped, present in roughly every photo
of me at a desk.
